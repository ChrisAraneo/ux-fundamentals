# Product development life cycle

## What is the product development life cycle?

The **product development life cycle** is the process a team follows to take a product from an idea to something people can actually use. Different companies describe it in slightly different ways, but most versions contain the same five stages: **brainstorm, define, design, test, and launch**.

UX designers take part in every stage, but they are most active during design and testing.

## The five stages

### 1. Brainstorm

The brainstorm stage is about **discovery and generating ideas**. The team asks who the users might be, what they need, and what problems they face. **Research is the most important activity here**, because good ideas come from understanding real people, not from guessing.

### 2. Define

In the define stage, the team uses what it learned to **narrow the focus**. It answers questions like: Who exactly is the product for? What will it do? Which features does it need, and which can wait?

### 3. Design

The first two stages are planning. In the design stage, UX designers **turn the insights into concrete designs**. They create many assets along the way, such as storyboards, sketches, wireframes, and prototypes.

### 4. Test

In the test stage, the team **checks the design with potential users** and collects feedback. Engineers also test whether the product works technically. Testing often sends the team back to earlier stages to improve the design.

### 5. Launch

Launch means **releasing the finished product to the public**. Launch is not the end of the story, though. Teams keep watching how people use the product and plan improvements for the next version.

## Example: a recipe app

Here is how the five stages could look for an app that helps people cook with what they already have at home:

1. **Brainstorm** - the team learns that many people throw away food because they don't know what to cook with leftovers.
2. **Define** - the team decides the app is for busy people living alone and that its core feature is "enter what's in your fridge, get a recipe".
3. **Design** - designers sketch the ingredient search, create wireframes, and build a clickable prototype.
4. **Test** - a few users try the prototype; most of them miss the search button, so the designers make it more visible.
5. **Launch** - the app is released, and the team starts collecting reviews and usage data for the next update.

::: tip Key takeaway
The product development life cycle has five stages: brainstorm, define, design, test, and launch. The stages often repeat, because testing almost always reveals something to improve.
:::
