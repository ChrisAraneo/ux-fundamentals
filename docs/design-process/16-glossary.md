# Glossary

Key terms from this chapter, explained in simple words and sorted alphabetically. For terms from the first chapter, see the [Chapter 1 glossary](../foundations/14-glossary).

## 0–D

- **5 Ws and H** - six questions (who, what, where, when, why, and how) that help you understand a problem before writing a problem statement.
- **Actionable insight** - a finding about users that the design can actually do something about.
- **Active listening** - giving your full attention to what someone says, understanding it, and remembering it, instead of planning your next question.
- **Aggregated empathy map** - an empathy map that combines several interviews with similar users and represents a whole user segment.
- **Call to action (CTA)** - the element, usually a button, that invites the user to take the main action on a screen.
- **Case study** - a portfolio piece that walks the reader through one project from the problem to the final design.
- **Competitive audit** - a structured review of competitors' strengths and weaknesses.
- **Crazy Eights** - an ideation exercise in which you sketch eight ideas in eight minutes; also called rapid sketching.
- **Curb cut** - a small ramp where the sidewalk meets the street.
- **Curb-cut effect** - when an improvement made for disabled people turns out to benefit many others too.
- **Customizable text** - a feature that lets users change the size, font, color, and spacing of text to read it more easily.
- **Demographics** - characteristics of a person or group, such as age, location, or occupation.
- **Designer bias** - the tendency to design for your own needs and preferences instead of the user's.
- **Direct competitor** - a company that offers a similar product to the same audience as you.
- **Domain** - the address of a website, such as `yourname.design`.

## E–H

- **Edge case** - a rare or unexpected situation that interrupts the normal user experience, often outside the user's control.
- **Empathy** - the ability to understand another person's thoughts and feelings from their point of view.
- **Empathy exercise** - imagining how a possible user would experience a situation, to prepare better research.
- **Empathy map** - a chart that summarizes what a user says, thinks, does, and feels.
- **Feedback loop** - the response a user gets after an action, which shows whether the action worked.
- **Goal statement** - a short summary of the chosen solution: the product, the action it enables, whom it helps, how it helps, and how success will be measured.
- **Hallway testing** - asking people in a public place to take part in quick research.
- **Happy path** - the ideal journey in which everything works and the user reaches their goal without problems.
- **Haptics** - vibrations or other touch-based feedback from a device.
- **Hick's law** - the principle that more options make it take longer to decide.
- **How might we (HMW)** - an exercise that turns a problem statement into many questions starting with "How might we...?" to spark ideas.
- **Human factors** - the abilities, characteristics, and limitations people bring to their interactions with products.
- **Hypothesis statement** - an educated guess about what might solve a user's problem.

## I–P

- **Ideation** - generating a broad set of ideas without judging them.
- **Indirect competitor** - a company that offers a similar product to a different audience, or a different product to the same audience.
- **Inference** - a conclusion supported by what you saw and heard, as opposed to an assumption.
- **Mental model** - a person's internal picture of how something works, which lets them predict what will happen.
- **Non-disclosure agreement (NDA)** - a contract in which you promise not to share confidential information.
- **One-user empathy map** - an empathy map based on a single interview.
- **Pain point** - a problem that frustrates users and stops them from getting what they need; it can be financial, product-related, process-related, or support-related.
- **Persona** - a fictional user, based on research, who represents a group of real users.
- **Personal brand** - the way your personality, skills, and values come together in how you present yourself as a designer.
- **Portfolio** - a collection of your work that shows your skills, usually as a website.
- **Problem statement** - a clear description of a user's need that the design should address.

## R–Z

- **Rapport** - a friendly, trusting connection that helps a participant feel relaxed and speak openly.
- **Representative sample** - a small group of participants whose characteristics reflect the larger group of real users.
- **Screener survey** - a short questionnaire that checks whether potential participants fit a study.
- **Serial position effect** - the tendency to remember the first and last items in a list better than the middle ones.
- **Sympathy** - recognizing someone's feelings and showing concern without sharing those feelings.
- **Unique value proposition** - the value your product offers that competitors don't.
- **User group** - a set of people with similar interests, goals, or concerns.
- **User journey** - every step and experience on a person's way to a particular goal.
- **User journey map** - a visual, step-by-step representation of a user journey, including actions, tasks, feelings, and opportunities.
- **User profile** - a short, fictional biography of a possible user, used for empathy exercises before research.
- **User segment** - a group of users with similar needs or behaviors.
- **User story** - a single invented sentence in a persona's voice that names the user, the action they want, and the benefit they expect.
- **Value proposition** - a short explanation of what a product does and why users should care.
- **Von Restorff effect** - the tendency to remember the item that looks different from similar items around it; also called the isolation effect.
- **WYSIWYG** - "what you see is what you get", an editor in which you edit content visually, exactly as it will appear.
