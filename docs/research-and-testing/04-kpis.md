# Key performance indicators

## What are KPIs?

**Key performance indicators (KPIs)** are measurable signs of progress toward a goal. In a research plan, they tell you how well a design is working. Imagine your manager asks, "How did the study go?" KPIs let you answer with facts, not just impressions.

Some KPIs should be as **high** as possible, others as **low** as possible. Choose the ones that best match your research goals.

## Common UX KPIs

### Time on task

**Time on task** is the time a participant needs to finish a task, for example submitting a form or booking a machine. Start a timer when the participant begins and stop it when they finish. In general, the shorter the time, the better the design.

### Use of navigation vs. search

This KPI compares how many people move through a product using its **navigation** (menus, tabs, links) and how many use the **search** function. Count clicks on navigation elements and compare them to the number of searches. Unlike most KPIs, there's no "good" or "bad" result. It shows users' preferences, so you can make both paths work well.

### User error rate

The **user error rate** shows where users make mistakes, for example tapping the wrong icon, forgetting to check a box, or entering information in the wrong format. These errors are **never the user's fault**; they point to parts of the design that need improvement. The fewer errors, the better. But be careful: a low error rate doesn't always mean a strong design. People can complete a task correctly without understanding what they did, which can be just as frustrating.

### Drop-off rate

The **drop-off rate** shows how many users give up before reaching the end of a task, such as a purchase or booking. People may quit because navigation is confusing, a step is frustrating, or they simply get bored. Your goal is to **lower** the drop-off rate with every iteration.

### Conversion rate

The **conversion rate** is the share of users who finish the action you want them to take, such as completing a booking. It's the opposite of the drop-off rate. You want it as **high** as possible. Comparing conversion rates between studies shows whether your design changes helped.

### System Usability Scale (SUS)

The **System Usability Scale (SUS)** is a standard questionnaire for measuring usability. Participants rate ten statements about a product on a five-point scale from "strongly disagree" to "strongly agree". Some statements are positive, such as "I thought the system was easy to use", and some are negative, such as "I found the system unnecessarily complex". The answers are turned into a score from 0 to 100. It's a quick and reliable way to check whether a design is working.

### Net Promoter Score (NPS)

The **Net Promoter Score (NPS)** measures how likely users are to recommend a product. Participants answer the question "How likely are you to recommend this product to a friend or colleague?" on a scale from 0 to 10:

- **Promoters (9–10)** - enthusiastic users who would recommend the product.
- **Passives (7–8)** - satisfied users who probably won't actively recommend it.
- **Detractors (0–6)** - unhappy users who might discourage others.

To calculate the NPS, take the percentage of promoters and subtract the percentage of detractors. The result ranges from -100 to 100.

*Example:* out of 20 participants, 10 are promoters (50%), 6 are passives, and 4 are detractors (20%). The NPS is 50 − 20 = **30**. A positive score is a good sign; a negative score suggests that the experience frustrates many users.

## Choosing KPIs

You rarely need all of these KPIs in one study. Choose two or three that match your research goals and the findings you'll want to present. Good KPIs:

- **Show progress toward your research goals.**
- **Reveal meaningful information about user behavior.**
- **Give actionable feedback about how the design performs.**

KPIs become even more useful in combination. If time on task is long, the error rate is high, and the conversion rate is low, the flow is clearly difficult and frustrating, and users are likely to give up.

### WashSlot KPIs

The WashSlot designer chooses four KPIs:

- **Time on task** - how long it takes to book a machine for a given day and time.
- **User error rate** - where residents make mistakes in the booking flow.
- **Conversion rate** - the percentage of participants who complete a booking.
- **SUS** - an overall usability score from a short questionnaire at the end.

::: tip Key takeaway
KPIs are measurable signs of how well a design works. Common UX KPIs are time on task, navigation vs. search, user error rate, drop-off rate, conversion rate, SUS, and NPS. Pick the few that best match your research goals and look at them together.
:::
