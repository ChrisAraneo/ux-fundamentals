# Glossary

Key terms from this chapter, explained in simple words and sorted alphabetically. For earlier terms, see the glossaries of [Chapter 1](../foundations/14-glossary) and [Chapter 2](../design-process/16-glossary).

## A–D

- **Attention economy** - the competition between products for people's limited time and attention.
- **Avatar** - a small image or icon, often a circle, that represents a user's profile.
- **Big-picture storyboard** - a storyboard that focuses on the user's experience, context, and emotions.
- **Bottom navigation bar** - a bar at the bottom of an app screen with icons for the main sections.
- **Canvas** - the large workspace in a design tool where all designs are placed.
- **Carousel** - a set of images or cards that users can swipe through one at a time.
- **Clickable prototype** - a digital prototype in which screens are connected, so users can click through them.
- **Close-up storyboard** - a storyboard that focuses on the product's screens, actions, and transitions.
- **Cognitive bias** - a systematic error in thinking that affects judgment and decisions.
- **Common region** - the Gestalt principle that elements inside the same closed area are seen as a group.
- **Confirmshaming** - a deceptive pattern that makes users feel guilty for declining an offer.
- **Connection node** - in Figma, the small circle on an element's edge used to create a prototype connection.
- **Deceptive patterns** - design techniques that push users into actions or purchases they never intended.
- **Design** - a solution to a real problem that users have.
- **Divider** - a thin line that separates items in a list or groups content.
- **Dominant culture** - the norms set by a society's most powerful groups, which are often treated as "normal".
- **Dopamine** - a chemical in the brain released when something pleasant happens, which makes us feel good.

## E–L

- **Elements** - the building blocks of a design, such as lines, shapes, and text.
- **Ethical design** - design that takes responsibility for its impact on people and society.
- **Fidelity** - how closely a design resembles the finished product in appearance and behavior.
- **Forced continuity** - a deceptive pattern in which a free trial turns into a paid subscription without warning.
- **Frame** - in a design tool, a container that usually represents one screen.
- **Functional prototype** - a prototype with realistic behavior, such as animations and small interactions, very close to the final product.
- **Gestalt principles** - principles that describe how people group elements, recognize patterns, and simplify what they see.
- **Hidden costs** - a deceptive pattern in which extra fees appear only at the end of checkout.
- **High fidelity (hi-fi)** - a detailed, polished design that is very close to the finished product.
- **Hotspot** - the element where a prototype interaction starts, such as a button.
- **Implicit bias** - unconscious attitudes and stereotypes about groups of people.
- **Industry standards** - common ways of drawing interface elements, such as lines for text and a box with an X for images.
- **Information architecture (IA)** - the way content is organized so users know where they are and where to find information.
- **Lorem ipsum** - scrambled Latin placeholder text used to show where text will go in a layout.
- **Low fidelity (lo-fi)** - a simple, rough design with little detail.
- **Low-fidelity prototype** - a simple interactive model that shows the basic structure and flow of a product.

## M–S

- **Marginalized groups** - people who experience discrimination or exclusion because of characteristics that society unfairly treats as inferior.
- **Multiversal design** - design that offers several ways to experience the same product, so different people can all use it fully; also called pluriversal design.
- **Pagination indicator** - small dots that show how many items a carousel has and which one is visible.
- **Paper prototype** - a low-fidelity prototype made of paper screens that a person switches by hand during testing.
- **Pre-mortem** - imagining at the start of a project that it has failed, to find possible causes early.
- **Prototype** - a preliminary, testable version of a product that demonstrates how it will work.
- **Proximity** - the Gestalt principle that elements close together are seen as related.
- **Scarcity** - a deceptive pattern that claims only a few items are left to pressure users.
- **Similarity** - the Gestalt principle that elements that look alike are seen as having the same function.
- **Sitemap** - a diagram that shows the structure of a product's pages and how they connect.
- **Sneak into basket** - a deceptive pattern in which extra items are added to a user's cart automatically.
- **Storyboard** - a series of drawn panels that shows a user's experience with a product.

## T–Z

- **Top bar** - a bar across the top of an app screen with the screen title and related actions.
- **Underrepresented groups** - people whose experiences and values are rarely represented in a society shaped by the dominant culture.
- **Urgency** - a deceptive pattern that uses artificial time pressure.
- **User flow** - the path a typical user follows to complete a task in a product, from start to finish.
- **Wireframe** - a basic outline of a screen made of lines, shapes, and text.
