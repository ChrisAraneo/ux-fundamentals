# Chapter 5: High-fidelity designs and prototypes

In [Chapter 3](../wireframes-and-prototypes/), you sketched wireframes and built a low-fidelity prototype. In [Chapter 4](../research-and-testing/), you tested it and improved the wireframes. Now your design gets its final look and feel. This chapter shows you how to turn wireframes into polished mockups, apply visual design principles, work with design systems, build a high-fidelity prototype, test it again, and hand the finished design to developers.

## What you will learn

1. **How to build mockups** - turning grayscale wireframes into screens with real type, color, icons, and images.
2. **How to make designs accessible** - readable text, sufficient contrast, and information that never depends on color alone.
3. **How to lay out a screen** - grids, containment, negative space, common screens, and navigation patterns.
4. **How to refine a design** - visual design principles such as emphasis, hierarchy, scale, and unity, plus more Gestalt principles.
5. **How to design consistently** - design systems, sticker sheets, and reusable components.
6. **How to build a high-fidelity prototype** - connections, interactions, animations, gestures, motion, and sound.
7. **How to finish a design** - testing and iterating on the prototype, deciding when it's done, handing it off, and writing a case study.

## Running example: WashSlot gets its final look

The **WashSlot** project continues. Residents of a 60-flat building use the app to check and book the shared washing machines in the basement laundry room. After the usability study in Chapter 4, the designer improved the wireframes: day tabs on the booking screen, large status labels, the remaining time for busy machines, and a reminder that is on by default.

The housing cooperative's board has approved the direction. Now the designer has to make WashSlot look and feel like a real app, test it once more, and prepare everything for the two freelance developers the cooperative has hired.

The personas you already know guide every visual decision: **Tomasz** (a busy father working from home), **Grace** (a retired librarian with a hearing aid who uses large text), and **Kofi** (a student working evening shifts who is still learning the local language).
