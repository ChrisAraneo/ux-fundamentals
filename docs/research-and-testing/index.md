# Chapter 4: UX research and usability testing

In [Chapter 3](../wireframes-and-prototypes/), you built wireframes and a low-fidelity prototype. Now it's time to find out whether your design actually works for the people it's meant for. This chapter shows you how to plan a research study, run usability studies, turn what you observe into insights, share those insights with your team, and improve your design based on them.

You learned the basics of UX research methods and research bias in [Chapter 1](../foundations/12-ux-research). This chapter turns that knowledge into a practical, step-by-step process.

## What you will learn

1. **How a research study works** - the four steps from planning to sharing results.
2. **How to communicate with participants** - respectful, clear, and inclusive language.
3. **How to plan research** - the seven elements of a research plan, including KPIs and the script.
4. **How to protect participants** - privacy, consent, and data safety.
5. **How to run usability studies** - moderated and unmoderated studies, moderation techniques, and reducing bias.
6. **How to make sense of data** - note-taking, affinity diagrams, themes, and insights.
7. **How to share and act on results** - presentations, reports, priorities, and design iterations.

## Running example: testing WashSlot

The **WashSlot** project continues. In Chapter 3, the designer built a low-fidelity prototype of the app that lets residents of a 60-flat building check and book the shared washing machines. The main flow is: open the app, see which machines are free, choose a machine and a time, add a reminder, and confirm the booking.

The housing cooperative is happy with the idea, but before any code is written, the team wants to know: **Can residents book a machine easily, without help?** In this chapter, you'll follow the designer as they plan a usability study, test the prototype with six residents, analyze the results, present them to the cooperative's board, and improve the wireframes.

The personas you already know, **Tomasz** (a busy father working from home), **Grace** (a retired librarian with a hearing aid who uses large text), and **Kofi** (a student working evening shifts who is still learning the local language), guide who is invited to the study.
