# Glossary

Key terms from this chapter, explained in simple words and sorted alphabetically.

## A–D

- **A/B testing** - comparing two versions of a design to find out which one works better.
- **Accessibility** - designing products, services, and environments so that people with disabilities can use them.
- **Alternative text (alt text)** - a short written description of an image or other visual element that screen readers can read aloud.
- **Assets** - all the building blocks of a design, such as texts, images, icons, and specifications for fonts, colors, sizes, and spacing.
- **Assistive technology (AT)** - products, equipment, or systems that make learning, working, and daily life easier for people with disabilities.
- **Bias** - a tendency to judge something too quickly, for or against it, without enough information.
- **Brand identity** - the visual style and voice that make a company recognizable.
- **Call to action (CTA)** - an element, usually a button, that invites the user to take a specific action, such as "Buy now".
- **Card sorting** - a research method where participants group topic cards into categories that make sense to them.
- **Color modification** - features that change screen colors to improve visibility, such as high-contrast mode or dark mode.
- **Competitive audit** - a review of competitors' products, their strengths, and their weaknesses.
- **Confirmation bias** - looking for evidence that supports what you already believe.
- **Cross-functional team** - a team made up of people with different skills, such as design, engineering, and product management.
- **Define** - the design thinking phase in which you decide which user problem to solve, based on research.
- **Design research** - research carried out while designing, to learn how the product should be built.
- **Design sprint** - a time-boxed process, usually five days, for solving one design challenge by designing, prototyping, and testing with users.
- **Design thinking** - a framework for solving problems with users in mind, made of five phases: empathize, define, ideate, prototype, and test.
- **Diary study** - a research method in which participants record their activities and experiences over a period of time.
- **Digital literacy** - a person's level of skill in using digital technologies and information.

## E–I

- **Empathize** - the design thinking phase in which you get to know your users through research.
- **Empathy** - the ability to understand another person's feelings and thoughts in their situation.
- **Empathy map** - a tool that organizes what users say, think, do, and feel.
- **Equality** - giving everyone the same amount of support or resources.
- **Equity** - giving each person the support they need to reach a fair outcome.
- **Equity-focused design** - design that pays special attention to groups that products have overlooked or excluded in the past.
- **False consensus bias** - assuming that other people think the same way you do.
- **Fidelity** - how closely a design resembles the final product.
- **Field study** - research carried out in the user's own environment.
- **Focus group** - a moderated discussion with a small group of people about a specific topic.
- **Foundational research** - research done before design that answers the question "What should we build?".
- **Framework** - a structure of steps and good practices that guides you through solving a problem.
- **Generalist** - a UX designer with a broad range of responsibilities.
- **Ideate** - the design thinking phase in which you generate many possible solutions.
- **Ideation** - generating a wide range of ideas without judging them.
- **Implicit bias** - unconscious attitudes and stereotypes we connect with groups of people.
- **Inclusive design** - design that considers personal characteristics such as ability, ethnicity, income, language, age, and gender.
- **Information architecture (IA)** - the structure of a product's content: how it is grouped, labeled, and connected.
- **Insight** - a discovery from research that changes how you understand users or their needs.
- **Interaction designer** - a designer who focuses on how a product behaves and flows.
- **Interview** - a research method for collecting in-depth information about people's opinions, experiences, and feelings.
- **Iteration** - repeating a process and improving the design a little each time.

## K–P

- **Key performance indicator (KPI)** - a measurable sign of progress toward a goal, such as the time needed to finish a task.
- **Motion designer** - a designer who focuses on movement and transitions in a product.
- **Persona** - a fictional character who represents a group of similar users.
- **Platform** - the medium on which people use a product, such as desktop, mobile, or smartwatch.
- **Post-launch research** - research carried out after release, to check whether the product works for users.
- **Primacy bias** - remembering the first participant or the first piece of information most strongly.
- **Primary research** - research you conduct yourself.
- **Problem statement** - a description of who the user is, what they need, and why, written from the designer's perspective.
- **Product** - a good, service, or feature that people use.
- **Product development life cycle** - the five stages a product goes through from first idea to release: brainstorm, define, design, test, and launch.
- **Production designer** - a designer who checks that designs are consistent and prepares them for engineers.
- **Prototype** - a test version of a product, from a paper sketch to a clickable model, used to try ideas with users.

## Q–Z

- **Qualitative research** - research based on observations and conversations that explains "why".
- **Quantitative research** - research based on numbers and measurements that explains "what" and "how many".
- **Recency bias** - remembering the most recent information most strongly.
- **Responsive web design** - a design approach in which a website's layout adapts automatically to the screen size.
- **Retrospective** - a team meeting after a sprint to discuss what went well and what to improve.
- **Screen reader** - software that reads aloud the text, interactive elements, and alt text on a screen.
- **Secondary research** - research based on information that someone else has collected.
- **Sitemap** - a diagram that shows the pages of a product and how they connect.
- **Specialist** - a designer who focuses deeply on one area of UX.
- **Speech to text** - software that turns spoken words into written text.
- **Sprint brief** - a document that helps participants prepare for a design sprint.
- **Startup** - a new company developing a unique product or service.
- **Sunk cost fallacy** - continuing something only because you have already invested time or money in it.
- **Survey** - a set of questions asked to many people to learn what most of them think.
- **Switch device** - assistive technology that replaces a keyboard or mouse.
- **T-shaped designer** - a designer with deep expertise in one area and broad knowledge of related areas.
- **Test** - the design thinking phase in which users try prototypes and you observe them.
- **Universal design** - creating one product that works for people with the widest possible range of abilities and situations.
- **Usability study** - a research method in which people use a product while you observe them, to find problems.
- **User** - anyone who interacts with a product.
- **User-centered design** - a design approach that puts the user's needs at the center of every decision.
- **User experience (UX)** - how a person feels while using a product.
- **User journey** - the series of steps and experiences a user goes through to reach a goal.
- **User story** - a one-sentence description of what a user wants and why, written from the user's point of view.
- **UX engineer** - an engineer who turns designs into working products while keeping the design intent.
- **UX program manager** - a person who keeps communication in a UX project clear and on schedule.
- **UX research** - observing and talking to users to understand what they do, what they need, and why.
- **UX researcher** - a person who runs studies and interviews to understand users.
- **UX writer** - a person who writes the text inside a product, such as labels and messages.
- **Visual designer** - a designer who focuses on how a product looks.
- **Voice control** - technology that lets people operate a device using only their voice.
- **Wireframe** - a simple sketch of a screen that shows its layout and structure.
