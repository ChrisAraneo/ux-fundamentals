# Glossary

Key terms from this chapter, explained in simple words and sorted alphabetically. For earlier terms, see the glossaries of [Chapter 1](../foundations/14-glossary), [Chapter 2](../design-process/16-glossary), and [Chapter 3](../wireframes-and-prototypes/14-glossary).

## A–C

- **A11y** - a short form of "accessibility", with 11 letters between the "a" and the "y".
- **Ableist language** - words that assume everyone has the same physical or mental abilities.
- **Acceptance testing** - testing how real users react to a finished product before or after launch.
- **Accessibility evaluation** - checking whether every part of a product meets accessibility standards.
- **Affinity** - a feeling of similarity or connection with something or someone.
- **Affinity diagram** - a method that organizes observations into groups with common themes.
- **Audit** - an outside review that checks whether research involving people was ethical and followed its plan.
- **Augmentative and alternative communication (AAC)** - devices or tools that help people communicate with pictures or symbols instead of speech.
- **Bug report** - a description of an error in a product, sent to the developers so they can fix it.
- **Click path** - the route a participant takes through a product to complete a task.
- **Concept testing** - testing an early idea, such as a sketch or a simple prototype, with users.
- **Conversion rate** - the share of users who finish the action you want them to take.

## D–K

- **Data recording** - documenting a study and its results consistently, following research standards.
- **Data retention** - how long research data is kept.
- **Data storage** - keeping research data safe from hacking and physical damage.
- **De-identification** - removing information that could identify participants from research data.
- **Discussion guide** - another name for the research script.
- **Drop-off rate** - the share of users who give up before completing a task.
- **Executive summary** - a short overview of the main conclusions at the beginning of a presentation or report.
- **Friendliness bias** - the tendency to agree with people we like, to keep a conversation pleasant.
- **Hawthorne effect** - the tendency to behave differently when you know you're being observed.
- **Incentive** - a reward that motivates people to take part in a study and thanks them for their time.
- **Insight** - a finding about people that shows users or their needs in a new light.
- **Key performance indicator (KPI)** - a measurable sign of progress toward a goal.

## L–P

- **Leading question** - a question worded to push someone toward a particular answer.
- **Methodology** - the steps taken to conduct research, collect data, and analyze it.
- **Moderated usability study** - a study in which a moderator guides participants in real time.
- **Moderator** - the person who guides participants through a moderated study.
- **Net Promoter Score (NPS)** - a measure of how likely users are to recommend a product, calculated as the percentage of promoters minus the percentage of detractors.
- **Non-disclosure agreement (NDA)** - a contract in which participants promise not to share confidential information they see during a study.
- **People-first language** - language that names the person before a condition, such as "a person who uses a wheelchair".
- **Personally identifiable information (PII)** - details that can identify a person, such as a name or email address.
- **Presentation** - a set of slides, each with new information; also called a deck.
- **Priority 0 (P0)** - an issue that has to be solved before the product can work properly, for example because it blocks the main flow, tricks users, or excludes people.
- **Project background** - a short explanation of why research is needed and which decisions depend on it.
- **Project stakeholders** - people involved in a project or affected by its results.

## Q–Z

- **Quality assurance (QA) testing** - checking a product for errors and making sure everything works as intended.
- **Representative sample** - a small group of participants that reflects key user groups and often-marginalized groups.
- **Research goals** - what a study aims to learn.
- **Research plan** - a document describing a study's background, goals, questions, KPIs, methodology, participants, and script.
- **Research questions** - the specific questions a study should answer; not the questions asked to participants.
- **Research report** - a document with the same information as a research presentation, but with fewer visuals.
- **Research study** - a structured, step-by-step investigation of a group of users and what they need.
- **Sample bias** - a distortion that happens when participants come from a narrow group that doesn't represent all users.
- **Script** - everything said during a research session: introduction, tasks, and questions.
- **Sensitive personally identifiable information (SPII)** - personal data that could cause harm, embarrassment, or discrimination if lost or stolen.
- **Social desirability bias** - the tendency to answer in a way that makes you look good to others.
- **Spreadsheet note-taking** - organizing study notes in a spreadsheet with columns for tasks, click paths, observations, quotes, and completion.
- **Synthesis** - combining observations to draw conclusions.
- **System Usability Scale (SUS)** - a ten-statement questionnaire that measures the usability of a design.
- **Think-aloud method** - asking participants to say what they're thinking while they use a product.
- **Time on task** - the time a participant needs to finish a task.
- **Unmoderated usability study** - a study in which participants complete tasks on their own, without a moderator.
- **Usability study** - a research method that checks how easily participants can complete key tasks in a design.
- **Usability task** - an activity participants try to complete during a usability study.
- **Use of navigation vs. search** - a comparison of how many people use a product's navigation and how many use its search.
- **User acceptance testing (UAT)** - testing that a product fulfills the purpose it was built for.
- **User error rate** - a measure of where and how often users make mistakes in a design.
- **Vulnerable groups** - people with a limited ability to give consent or with special privacy concerns.
