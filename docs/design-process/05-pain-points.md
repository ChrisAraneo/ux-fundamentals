# Pain points

## What is a pain point?

A **pain point** is any problem in the user experience that frustrates users and stops them from getting what they need. Finding and solving pain points is at the heart of UX design. Empathy and research are the tools that help you find them.

A classic example of a pain point is a door with a handle that suggests "pull" when you actually have to push. Such doors are often called **Norman doors**, after the design researcher Don Norman, who used them to show how design can confuse people. If the door doesn't do what its shape suggests, users get stuck.

## Needs users know and needs they don't know

Some needs are easy to discover, because users can describe them: "I want the app to load faster." Other needs are hidden, because users can't imagine a solution that doesn't exist yet. Before streaming services existed, few people would have said, "I want to watch any film instantly without owning it." Good research looks for both kinds of needs, by listening to what users say and observing what they actually struggle with.

## Four types of pain points

Most pain points fit into one of four categories:

1. **Financial** - problems related to money, for example unexpected fees at checkout, a paywall in the middle of an article, or a subscription that is hard to cancel.
2. **Product** - problems with the quality of the product itself, for example an app that crashes, a feature that doesn't work, or a confusing door handle.
3. **Process** - problems with the steps users must go through, for example a checkout that requires creating an account or a form that loses all data after an error.
4. **Support** - problems with getting help, for example a hidden phone number, a chatbot that can't understand the question, or slow replies from customer service.

### WashSlot example

The WashSlot designer sorts the pain points found in the interviews:

- **Financial** - residents who forget their laundry pay for an extra cycle when a neighbor restarts the machine with their clothes still inside.
- **Product** - one dryer often stops in the middle of a cycle without any warning.
- **Process** - residents carry heavy baskets downstairs without knowing if a machine is free, and the paper sheet doesn't show real availability.
- **Support** - nobody knows whom to contact when a machine breaks, so broken machines stay out of order for weeks.

## Pain points and empathy

Remember that the same problem can be small for one person and a serious barrier for another. A heavy laundry basket on the stairs is an annoyance for Kofi, but it might be dangerous for Grace. Always ask whose pain you are looking at, and think about users with different abilities. Tools like [empathy maps](./04-empathy-maps) and [personas](./06-personas) help you see pain points from many perspectives.

::: tip Key takeaway
Pain points are problems that frustrate users and block their goals. Look for needs that users can describe and needs they can't, and sort pain points into four categories: financial, product, process, and support.
:::
