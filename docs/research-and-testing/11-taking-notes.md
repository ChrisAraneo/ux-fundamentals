# Taking notes

## Why take notes if the session is recorded?

Even when a session is recorded, notes are essential:

- **They capture what recordings miss** - your thoughts in the moment, participants' facial expressions, hesitation, and tone of voice.
- **They summarize each session** - a short summary of how each participant experienced the product makes comparison easier.
- **They collect strong quotes** - memorable sentences that bring your presentation to life.
- **They save time** - there's rarely time to rewatch every recording from start to finish.

Be careful when interpreting body language and tone. A frown may mean confusion, or concentration, or nothing at all. In a moderated study, ask the participant how they felt at that moment instead of guessing.

### Example: the same task, two very different tones

Two WashSlot participants struggled with the same task: booking a machine for Thursday.

- **Participant 2** said in an irritated voice: *"Why can I only see today? This is annoying. I'd give up and just go downstairs."*
- **Participant 5** said cheerfully: *"Hmm, no Thursday here... let me try the little icon up there. Oh, there it is! Funny place for it."*

Both had the same problem, but their emotions were very different. Good notes capture both the problem and the feeling.

## Who takes notes?

On many teams, a UX researcher runs the study and takes notes. But it's very useful when other team members also watch and take notes, because each person notices different things. A UX writer may notice that a label confused someone, a designer may notice how long it took to find a button, and an engineer may notice a technical issue. After the study, the team combines everyone's notes and turns them into insights.

As a beginner, you won't be expected to run studies alone, but you'll often be asked to observe and take notes.

## Ways to take notes

There's no single right way. Common options are:

- **One sheet of paper** - quick, but notes get messy and hard to organize later.
- **Sticky notes** - one observation per note, which makes it easy to group them later in an [affinity diagram](./12-from-observations-to-insights).
- **A spreadsheet** - a structured method that keeps all notes from all participants organized in one place.

## Spreadsheet note-taking

**Spreadsheet note-taking** organizes observations into columns. Create one tab per participant and fill in the task column before the study starts. Typical columns are:

1. **Task** - the participant's name or number and each task from the script.
2. **Click path** - the route the participant took to complete the task, for example "Home > Machine 2 > Booking > Calendar icon > Thursday > 19:00". The first few clicks are especially telling, because they show whether the navigation feels intuitive.
3. **Observations** - behaviors, opinions, attitudes, errors, and moments of confusion, written by you as indirect observations.
4. **Quotes** - important things participants said, both positive and negative, word for word.
5. **Task completion** - how easily the task was completed, for example: **1** = completed easily, **2** = completed with difficulty, **3** = not completed. Use "N/A" for questions that aren't tasks.

Add a section at the bottom for additional notes, such as comments participants make after the tasks.

### Example: notes for one WashSlot participant

| Task | Click path | Observations | Quotes | Completion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Book Thursday at 19:00 | Home > Machine 2 > Booking > scrolled > Calendar icon > Thursday > 19:00 > Confirm | Didn't see the calendar icon at first; scrolled the time list looking for other days | "Why can I only see today?" | 2 |
| 2. Get notified when laundry is ready | Summary > Confirm | Didn't notice the reminder switch on the summary screen | "I'd expect it to remind me automatically." | 3 |
| 3. What to do if a machine is still full | Home > Menu > Settings > House rules | Looked in the menu first, found the rules in settings | "House rules in settings? I'd never look there." | 2 |
| 4. Move booking to Friday | Home > My bookings > Thursday booking > Change > Friday | Completed quickly | "That was easy." | 1 |
| 5. Describe the app to a neighbor | N/A | Positive overall, but worried about older neighbors | "Handy, but my mum would struggle with the tiny text." | N/A |

When every participant's tab is complete, it becomes easy to see which tasks were difficult for most people and which behaviors repeated.

::: tip Key takeaway
Take notes even when you record, because notes capture emotions, quick summaries, and quotes. Spreadsheet note-taking organizes each task's click path, observations, quotes, and completion score in one place, which makes analysis much easier later.
:::
