# Ethical and inclusive design

## What is ethical design?

**Ethical design** means understanding how your design work affects people and the world, and taking responsibility for that impact. The products you design become part of people's lives. They should help people, give them real choices, and be honest about their purpose. They should not:

- **Control users** - take over their time or attention.
- **Encourage addiction** - create habits that harm users.
- **Create unhealthy relationships** - make people feel unable to put their phone away.
- **Take advantage of users** - exploit their trust, data, or weaknesses.

## Dominant culture and who gets left out

In every society, the most privileged and powerful groups tend to shape the **dominant culture**: the values and norms that are treated as "normal". When the dominant culture's point of view is the only one considered in design, other people are easily left out.

Two groups deserve special attention:

- **Marginalized groups** - people who experience discrimination or exclusion because of characteristics or life experiences that society unfairly treats as inferior. Examples include disabled people, people with little or no internet access, and speakers of minority languages.
- **Underrepresented groups** - people whose experiences and values are rarely represented in a society shaped by the dominant culture, for example people of certain genders or sexual orientations, people of color, and ethnic minorities.

These groups aren't part of the dominant culture, so designers often forget about them unless they make a conscious effort to include them.

*Example:* someone without a computer at home should be able to fill in a job application on a phone just as easily as on a laptop. And if your mental picture of a "family" is two parents and two children, you may forget single parents, grandparents raising grandchildren, or several generations living together.

## Questions to challenge your assumptions

Use these questions throughout a project:

- **In what ways are your users different from you?**
- **How can people outside the dominant culture feel more included in your design?**
- **What difficulties might specific groups face when using your product?**
- **What is the opposite of how you feel about your design?** - someone among your users probably feels that way.
- **How can you avoid treating the dominant culture as the default?**
- **How can your design invite every perspective?**

## Edge cases and bias

As you learned in [Chapter 2](../design-process/07-user-stories-and-edge-cases), an **edge case** is a situation the designers didn't prepare for, which users can't fix themselves. Be careful with the label "edge case": it can hide bias. A group may only look rare because nobody from it was included in research. Plan for problems that different groups might face, and be ready to change your design when you learn about needs you didn't consider.

## Inclusive design in practice

**Inclusive design** means making design choices that consider characteristics such as ability, ethnicity, income, language, age, and gender. In inclusive design, there is no "normal" or "average" user. The goal is that no one is excluded from using a product because their needs were ignored.

### Build a diverse team

A team of researchers and designers with different backgrounds, including people from groups that are often excluded, brings many perspectives into every phase of the design process.

### Be accountable

Ask for the opinions of users, colleagues, and stakeholders who are different from you, and include their insights in your designs.

### Create inclusive personas

Include marginalized and underrepresented users in your personas, so you don't design only for people who seem "typical" to you. Use practical characteristics such as age, gender, and ability, but stay flexible and update personas when you notice their limits.

### Broaden your definition of "stakeholder"

Traditionally, stakeholders are project leaders, managers, and investors. In a broader sense, a **stakeholder** is anyone or anything a project can affect, including people who never use the product directly. In WashSlot, stakeholders include not only residents and the housing cooperative, but also the caretaker who fixes machines and the neighbors who are disturbed by late-night washing.

### Collaborate widely

The more you work with the different people your product affects, the better your insights and your product will be. Check whether small design details invite all perspectives, and remember that even universal design principles need to be adapted to new contexts.

### Consider multiversal design

**Universal design** looks for one solution that works for as many people as possible. **Multiversal** (or pluriversal) design accepts that different people may need different ways to experience the same thing. A multiversal design offers more than one entry point or way to use it. For example, a sighted person and a blind person should both get the full experience, through different means.

*WashSlot example:* residents can book a machine in the app, on a shared tablet in the laundry room, or by asking the caretaker. Each path gives the same result.

### Design for people coming online

Many people around the world are just starting to use the internet. They may have little experience with smartphones, limited data plans, or no content in their language. Designing for them means simple flows, light pages, clear language, and experiences that don't assume previous knowledge.

## A journey that never ends

Designing ethically and inclusively isn't a checklist you complete once. It requires continuous learning, listening, and improvement. By learning these ideas early in your career, you can help move the industry in the right direction.

::: tip Key takeaway
Ethical design means taking responsibility for your work's impact. Look beyond the dominant culture, include marginalized and underrepresented groups, question your assumptions, broaden who counts as a stakeholder, and offer more than one way to use your product.
:::
