# Glossary

Key terms from this chapter, explained in simple words and sorted alphabetically. For earlier terms, see the glossaries of [Chapter 1](../foundations/14-glossary), [Chapter 2](../design-process/16-glossary), [Chapter 3](../wireframes-and-prototypes/14-glossary), and [Chapter 4](../research-and-testing/15-glossary).

## A–C

- **60-30-10 rule** - a guideline for balancing a palette: about 60% neutral color, 30% secondary color, and 10% accent color.
- **Accessibility annotations** - notes on a design that tell developers the heading levels and the order in which elements should be read and focused.
- **Action** - in a prototype, what happens after a trigger, for example navigating to another screen or opening an overlay.
- **Affordance** - a visual cue that shows what an element does and how to use it.
- **Asymmetry** - a lack of balance between parts of a design, used to draw attention or add interest.
- **Auto Layout** - a Figma feature that makes a frame grow or shrink with its content while keeping the set spacing.
- **Basic grid** - lines crossing at equal distances that divide a screen into small squares; Figma calls it a uniform grid.
- **Border** - a containment method that uses a continuous line around content to set it apart.
- **Case study** - a short, visual story of a design project, including its goal, your role, the process, and the outcome.
- **Closure** - a Gestalt principle: people mentally complete incomplete shapes to see a whole.
- **Color blindness** - difficulty telling certain colors apart; also called color vision deficiency.
- **Color palette** - the limited set of colors used across a product.
- **Component** - a reusable design element, such as a button or a card; in Figma, an element saved with "Create component".
- **Constraints** - Figma settings that control how an element behaves when its frame is resized.
- **Containment** - using visual boundaries (dividers, borders, fill, and shadows) to keep a design organized.
- **Continuity** - a Gestalt principle: elements arranged along a line or curve appear related.
- **Contrast** - a clear difference between elements, such as light and dark or large and small, used for emphasis and readability.
- **Contrast ratio** - a measure of the difference in brightness between text and its background, from 1:1 to 21:1.
- **Copy** - the written text in a design.

## D–K

- **Design system** - a collection of reusable styles, components, guidelines, and code that helps teams build consistent products.
- **Design tokens** - named values, such as "color-accent" or "spacing-16", that designers and developers share.
- **Divider** - a containment method that uses a single line to separate sections or items.
- **Drop shadow** - a shadow outside an element that makes it look raised above the page.
- **Emphasis** - making an element attract attention.
- **Fill** - a containment method that colors the background of a shape or section.
- **Font** - one specific size, weight, and style of a typeface.
- **Gesture** - a way of interacting with a device by touching its screen, such as a tap, swipe, or pinch.
- **Gradient** - a fill that fades gradually from one color to another.
- **Gutter** - the gap between the columns of a layout grid; also called an alley.
- **Handoff** - passing finished designs, specs, and assets to developers, who build the product.
- **Hero image** - a large, eye-catching image at the top of a page.
- **Hierarchy** - a visual design principle that arranges elements in order of importance.
- **High-fidelity prototype** - a detailed, interactive model that looks and works almost like the final product.
- **Hint text** - light example text inside an input that shows the expected format; also called ghost text.
- **Hue** - the color family, such as red, green, or blue.
- **Iconography** - the system of icons a product uses.
- **Inner shadow** - a shadow inside an element that makes it look pressed in.
- **Input** - a field where users enter information; also called a text field.
- **Instance** - a copy of a main component that updates when the main component changes.
- **Interactivity** - the connections and reactions that make a prototype work.
- **Intrinsic interest** - how eye-catching an element is on its own, such as a photo or animation.

## L–P

- **Layout** - the way elements are arranged on a screen.
- **Layout grid** - a set of columns separated by gutters, with margins, used to align elements.
- **Legibility** - how easy it is to tell individual letters apart.
- **Light sensitivity** - discomfort caused by bright light or bright colors; also called photophobia.
- **Low contrast sensitivity** - seeing objects as dim or blurry, which makes low-contrast content hard to read.
- **Main component** - the original component in Figma; changes to it update all its instances.
- **Margin** - empty space outside an element or between the content and the edge of the screen.
- **Mockup** - a static, high-fidelity picture of a screen that shows what the final product will look like.
- **Motion** - animating design elements to guide attention and explain what's happening.
- **Navigation bar** - a bar with links to a product's most important sections, often at the top or bottom of the screen.
- **Navigation hub** - a home screen with links to all main sections, to which users return to change sections.
- **Navigation menu** - a hidden menu that opens from an icon of three lines; also called a hamburger menu.
- **Negative space** - the empty space around and between elements; also called white space.
- **Onboarding** - screens that introduce new users to an app's main features.
- **Opacity** - how solid or see-through a color is.
- **Orientation** - the direction of a layout, such as portrait (vertical) or landscape (horizontal).
- **Padding** - the space between an element's content and its edge.
- **Perceived physical weight** - the impression that an element is heavy or light.
- **Proportion** - the balance between elements whose sizes have changed.

## Q–Z

- **Readability** - how easy it is to read words, sentences, and paragraphs.
- **Saturation** - how intense or pure a color is.
- **Scalable** - able to keep working well when the workload or the team grows.
- **Scale** - the size of an element in relation to other elements.
- **Shadow** - a containment method that creates a feeling of depth, often with borders or fill.
- **State** - a visual version of a component that shows its current status, such as pressed or disabled.
- **Sticker sheet** - a collection of a design's most-used elements and components in one place; also called a design kit.
- **Style guide** - a document that describes a product's visual style.
- **Symmetry** - a Gestalt principle: balanced or mirrored parts look stable, orderly, and related.
- **Tab bar** - a row of tabs that switches between views of related content.
- **Text style** - a saved set of font settings that can be reused and updated everywhere at once.
- **Trigger** - in a prototype, the user's action that starts an interaction, such as a tap.
- **Type classification** - a broad family of type styles, such as serif and sans serif.
- **Type scale** - a set of text sizes that increase step by step, from labels to the largest heading.
- **Typeface** - the overall design of a set of letters, such as their stroke shapes and serifs.
- **Typographic hierarchy** - the order of importance created with different fonts, showing users where to look first.
- **Typography** - the craft of arranging text so it's readable, clear, and pleasant to look at.
- **UI kit** - a file with ready-made interface components that designers can copy into their designs.
- **Unity** - how well the elements of a design work together to express one idea.
- **Value** - how light or dark a color is.
- **Variants** - a Figma feature that groups the states and versions of a component into one set.
- **Variety** - using different elements to break monotony and create interest.
- **Visual balance** - the feeling that a design's visual weight is evenly distributed around its center of attention.
- **Visual design** - how a product looks to the people who use it.
- **Visual weight** - the power an element has to attract the eye.
- **Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG)** - international guidelines for making digital content accessible to people with disabilities.
