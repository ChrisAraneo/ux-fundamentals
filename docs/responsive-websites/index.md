# Chapter 6: Responsive websites

In [Chapter 5](../high-fidelity-design/), you finished a mobile app: polished mockups, a tested high-fidelity prototype, and a case study. This chapter takes the same project to the web. You'll design a **responsive website**, a website whose layout adapts to the screen it's shown on, from a wide desktop monitor to a narrow phone. Along the way, you'll revisit every step of the design process, this time with the extra challenge of many screen sizes. The chapter ends with the people side of design work: critiques, developers, your portfolio, and your first job search.

## What you will learn

1. **What makes a website responsive** - how responsive web apps differ from apps you install, and why companies need both.
2. **How to structure a website** - the four common website structures, sitemaps, and homepage layouts.
3. **How to wireframe for many screens** - paper wireframes, plotting layouts on grid paper, breakpoints, and components that save space.
4. **How to go digital** - digital wireframes, accessibility refinements, and Gestalt checks.
5. **How to bring the website to life** - a low-fidelity prototype, mockups for desktop and mobile, and a high-fidelity prototype.
6. **How to make the web accessible** - WCAG, focus order, heading tags, labels, and zoom.
7. **How to work with other people** - design critiques, working with front-end and back-end developers, and a case study for your portfolio.
8. **How to start your career** - UX job titles, types of employers, and a designer's resume.

## Running example: WashSlot goes online

The **WashSlot** app has launched. Residents of the 60-flat building now check and book the four washing machines and two dryers from their phones, and the arguments about who is next have mostly stopped.

But not everyone is on board. Some residents don't own a smartphone, some have phones with too little storage for another app, and a few simply prefer a bigger screen. The housing cooperative also wants to put a shared tablet on the wall of the laundry room, as promised in the app's case study. So the board asks the designer for a **WashSlot website** that works in any browser, on any device.

The website must do everything the app does (show live machine status, book a slot, and manage bookings), plus a few things that suit a website better: the house rules, a guide to the machines, notices from the cooperative, and a page for reporting problems.

The personas from earlier chapters still guide every decision:

- **Tomasz, 41** - a single father who works from home and now wants to book the machines from his laptop between video calls.
- **Grace, 74** - a retired librarian with a hearing aid who uses large text and reads on her tablet; she never installed the app.
- **Kofi, 23** - a student working evening shifts who is still learning the local language; his old phone is almost full, so he'd rather use a website than install another app.
