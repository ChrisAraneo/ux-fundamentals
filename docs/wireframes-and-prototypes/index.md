# Chapter 3: Wireframes and low-fidelity prototypes

In [Chapter 2](../design-process/), you learned how to understand users, define their problems, and generate ideas. This chapter is about turning those ideas into something people can see and click. You will plan how users move through a product, sketch its screens, organize its content, and build your first simple prototypes, on paper and in a design tool.

You will also learn about the responsibility that comes with this work: how bias can sneak into designs, which tricks designers should never use, and how to design ethically and inclusively.

## What you will learn

1. **How ideas become prototypes** - the steps between a goal statement and a clickable model.
2. **How to plan the experience** - user flows and storyboards.
3. **How to sketch screens** - fidelity, wireframes, and the standard way to draw interface elements.
4. **How to organize content** - information architecture and sitemaps.
5. **How to wireframe on paper and digitally** - from quick sketches to clean digital wireframes in Figma.
6. **How people perceive layouts** - the Gestalt principles of similarity, proximity, and common region.
7. **How to build low-fidelity prototypes** - paper prototypes and clickable prototypes in Figma.
8. **How to design responsibly** - bias in design, deceptive patterns, the attention economy, and ethical, inclusive design.

## Running example: WashSlot continues

This chapter continues the **WashSlot** project from Chapter 2: an app that lets residents of a 60-flat apartment building check and book the shared washing machines in the basement laundry room. You already know its three personas:

- **Tomasz, 41** - a single father who works from home and wants to know if a machine is free before going downstairs.
- **Grace, 74** - a retired librarian with a hearing aid who needs a visible signal when her laundry is done.
- **Kofi, 23** - a student working evening shifts who does laundry late at night and is still learning the local language.

You also have the goal statement from the ideation phase:

> Our WashSlot app lets residents who share a laundry room check machine availability and book a slot in advance, which helps them by saving unnecessary trips and reducing conflicts between neighbors. We'll know it works when weekly bookings grow and residents report fewer wasted trips in a monthly survey.

Now it's time to design the app itself.
