# Glossary

Key terms from this chapter, explained in simple words and sorted alphabetically. For earlier terms, see the glossaries of [Chapter 1](../foundations/14-glossary), [Chapter 2](../design-process/16-glossary), [Chapter 3](../wireframes-and-prototypes/14-glossary), [Chapter 4](../research-and-testing/15-glossary), and [Chapter 5](../high-fidelity-design/18-glossary).

## A–C

- **Above the fold** - the part of a web page that's visible without scrolling.
- **Advertising agency** - a team of creatives that builds marketing campaigns for clients.
- **Annotation** - a note added to a design that explains something the visuals can't show, such as focus order, heading levels, or labels.
- **Apprenticeship** - paid, on-the-job training alongside an experienced professional, usually lasting one to two years.
- **ARIA** - Accessible Rich Internet Applications; a set of attributes developers add to web elements to give assistive technology extra information.
- **Asymmetrical layout** - a layout that is intentionally unbalanced to draw attention to certain elements.
- **Back-end developer** - a developer who writes the code behind a website: its architecture and how data is stored and retrieved.
- **Below the fold** - the part of a web page that users see only after scrolling.
- **Box layout** - a layout made of independent boxes of different sizes, each showing its own item or category.
- **Breakpoint** - a screen width at which a responsive layout changes to a different arrangement.
- **Call to action (CTA)** - an element, usually a button, that prompts users to take a specific action, such as "Sign up" or "Book".
- **Card** - a compact surface that previews content and actions about one subject.
- **Carousel** - a strip of images or cards that users move through by clicking, swiping, or waiting for it to loop; also called a slideshow.
- **Case study** - a short, visual presentation of a design project that covers its goal, your role, the process, and the outcome.
- **Child page** - a page in a sitemap that sits below a more general parent page.
- **Cognitive overload** - a state in which too much information or too many tasks at once make it hard for people to complete anything.
- **Common region** - a Gestalt principle: elements inside the same boundary seem to belong together.
- **Corporation** - a large company with thousands of employees working on many projects.

## D–F

- **Database model** - a website structure that combines an organized collection of information with search and filtering.
- **Dedicated mobile app** - an app installed on a device from an app store and opened from an icon; also called a native app.
- **Deep sitemap** - a sitemap with five or more levels, typical for very large websites.
- **Design agency** - a company that designs the look and experience of brands, products, and services for clients.
- **Design critique session** - a planned meeting in which designers present their work and receive feedback from teammates; often called a crit.
- **Desktop first** - designing the large layout first and then adapting it for smaller screens.
- **Entry-level job** - a role that doesn't require previous experience in the field.
- **Entry point** - the page where a user flow begins.
- **Eye-tracking pattern** - the path that people's eyes typically follow across a page, such as an F or a Z shape.
- **F-shaped pattern** - a scanning pattern in which people read across the top, then a little lower, then down the left side; suits text-heavy pages.
- **Facilitator** - the person who runs a critique session, keeps time, and makes sure everyone is heard.
- **Featured image layout** - a layout in which one large image or video fills most of the screen; also called a single image layout.
- **Feedback** - other people's ideas about what is and isn't working in a design.
- **Flat sitemap** - a sitemap with no more than four levels, typical for smaller websites.
- **Flyout menu** - a panel of options that slides in from the screen edge or appears next to a menu item.
- **Focal point** - a distinct area of a page that catches the eye first.
- **Focus order** - the sequence in which users move through the interactive elements of a page with a keyboard or screen reader; also called traversal order.
- **Footer** - the area at the bottom of every page with secondary links, such as contact details and the privacy policy.
- **Freelancer** - a designer who works for themselves and finds their own clients.
- **Front-end developer** - a developer who writes the code for everything users see and interact with in the browser; also called a web developer.

## G–L

- **Generalist** - a designer with a broad range of responsibilities.
- **Grid of cards layout** - a layout that shows a list of similar items as cards of the same size, such as search results or videos.
- **Grid paper** - paper printed with evenly spaced dots or squares, used to plot layouts in proportion.
- **Hamburger menu** - a navigation menu hidden behind an icon of three horizontal lines.
- **Header** - the area at the top of a web page, usually with the logo, the navigation, and account or cart icons.
- **Heading** - a title or subtitle that starts a page, section, or group of content.
- **Heading tags** - HTML elements from H1 to H6 that tell browsers and screen readers the level of each heading.
- **Hierarchical model** - a website structure that organizes pages from general to specific, like a family tree.
- **Hover state** - the way a link or button changes when a mouse pointer is over it.
- **HTML** - the language used to structure the content of web pages.
- **Initial focus** - where a page directs the user's attention when it opens, to help them start their task.
- **Interaction designer** - a designer who focuses on how a product works and flows.
- **Internship** - a temporary position with a narrow set of tasks, done under supervision.
- **Key terms** - words that repeat in a job posting and reveal what the role really involves.
- **Label** - the text assistive technology reads out for an interactive element; also called a screen reader verbalization.
- **Landmark** - a marked region of a page, such as the navigation, main content, or footer, that assistive technology can jump to.
- **Lazy loading** - loading more content, such as cards, only when the user scrolls close to it.
- **Lockup** - a group of an image and text that works as one unit, such as a product photo with its name and price.
- **Lorem ipsum** - meaningless placeholder text that shows where real text will go.

## M–R

- **Matrix model** - a website structure in which content is linked in many directions, so users choose their own path; also called the web model.
- **Mobile first** - designing the smallest layout first and adding content and columns as the screen grows.
- **Motion designer** - a designer who focuses on how it feels to move through a product, using transitions and animation.
- **Multicolumn layout** - a layout with two or more columns of content.
- **Notetaker** - the person who records all feedback during a critique session.
- **Parent page** - a main category page in a sitemap that has more specific child pages below it.
- **Personal brand** - the consistent way you present yourself as a designer, through your style, colors, logo, and voice.
- **Pop-up search bar** - a search field that appears only when users tap a search icon, used to save space on small screens.
- **Presenter** - the designer who shares their work during a critique session.
- **Product designer** - a designer responsible for how the whole product comes together, across visual and interaction design.
- **Reflow** - the way content rearranges itself to fit the available width, for example when users zoom in.
- **Responsive web app** - a website that adapts to the user's device and opens in a web browser.
- **Responsive web design** - an approach in which a website's layout changes automatically to fit the screen.
- **Reviewer** - a person who gives feedback on a design during a critique session.

## S–Z

- **Semantic role** - the type of an interactive element, such as button, link, or checkbox, as assistive technology announces it.
- **Sequential model** - a website structure that guides users step by step in a fixed order.
- **Single-column layout** - a layout with all content in one vertical column.
- **Sitemap** - a diagram that shows how the pages of a website or app are prioritized, linked, and named.
- **Specialist** - a designer who builds deep expertise in one area, for example motion design.
- **Startup** - a young company developing a unique product or service, usually with a small team and a tight budget.
- **Sticky navigation** - a navigation bar that stays at the top of the screen while users scroll; also called fixed navigation.
- **Tiered layer cake layout** - a layout of stacked horizontal rows, each with its own number of columns.
- **Traversal order** - see focus order.
- **UI designer** - a designer focused on the look and function of a product's interface.
- **UI kit** - a ready-made collection of components and styles for a design tool.
- **Visual designer** - a designer focused on how a product looks.
- **WCAG** - the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines from the W3C, which describe how to make websites and other web content usable for people with disabilities.
- **WebAIM** - Web Accessibility in Mind, an organization that offers accessibility resources and a widely used contrast checker.
- **Wireframe** - a simple outline of a screen that shows where content goes, without visual detail.
- **Z-shaped pattern** - a scanning pattern in which people look across the top, diagonally down to the left, and across again; suits pages with little text.
