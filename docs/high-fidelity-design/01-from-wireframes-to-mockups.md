# From wireframes to mockups

## What is visual design?

**Visual design** is how a product looks to the people who use it. It includes the typography, colors, icons, images, and the way all of these are arranged on a screen. Good visual design isn't decoration. It helps people understand what's on the screen, find what they need, and trust the product.

So far, your WashSlot screens have been grayscale boxes and lines. That was on purpose: low-fidelity designs let you focus on structure and flow without getting distracted by details. Now that testing has confirmed the structure, it's time to add the visual layer.

## Fidelity, mockups, and prototypes

As a reminder, **fidelity** describes how closely a design matches the final product. A **low-fidelity (lo-fi)** design is simple and rough. A **high-fidelity (hi-fi)** design is detailed and polished, and it looks almost like the finished product.

Two hi-fi artifacts are the focus of this chapter:

- **Mockup** - a static, high-fidelity picture of a screen. It shows exactly what the final product will look like, but you can't click or tap anything.
- **High-fidelity prototype** - mockups connected into an interactive model. It looks and behaves almost like the real app, so people can try it, give feedback, and approve it.

You can think of it this way: wireframes answer "what goes where?", mockups answer "what does it look like?", and a hi-fi prototype answers "how does it feel to use?".

## Organizing a Figma file

Before you design a single mockup, set up your Figma file so you and your teammates can find things quickly. A few terms matter here:

- **File** - one Figma document, for example "WashSlot app".
- **Page** - a section inside a file. Each page has its own **canvas**, the large working area where you place your designs.
- **Frame** - a container on the canvas, usually the size of one device screen. Each screen you design lives in its own frame.

### How to add pages and frames

1. **Add a page** - in the left panel, click the plus icon next to the list of pages, then double-click the new page to rename it.
2. **Choose the Frame tool** - select it in the toolbar or press F (or A) on your keyboard.
3. **Pick a size** - in the right panel, choose a preset for a phone, tablet, or desktop, or drag on the canvas to draw a frame of any size.
4. **Name the frame** - double-click the frame's name and give it a clear name, such as "01 Home".

### A tidy file structure

A common setup is one page per stage of your work, with frames named and numbered in the order users see them. Numbers make the user flow visible at a glance, and they make the list of frames in the left panel easy to navigate, even when the canvas grows very large.

*WashSlot example:* the designer creates five pages in the WashSlot file: "Wireframes", "Mockups", "Sticker sheet", "Prototype", and "Archive". On the "Mockups" page, the frames are named "01 Home", "02 Booking", "03 Summary", "04 Confirmation", and "05 My bookings".

::: tip Pro tip
You can open several Figma files in separate tabs. This lets you look at a reference file, such as your wireframes or a design system, without closing the file you're working on.
:::

## What makes a mockup?

When you turn a wireframe into a mockup, you replace placeholders with real visual decisions:

1. **Typography** - the typefaces, sizes, and weights that make text readable and show what's most important.
2. **Color** - a palette that expresses the brand, creates emphasis, and keeps enough contrast.
3. **Icons and images** - symbols that save space and pictures that tell a story.
4. **Layout** - grids, containment, and negative space that hold everything in place.

The next pages cover each of these in turn. Keep in mind that the skills are not limited to Figma. Every design tool has pages, frames, text styles, and color styles under slightly different names, so what you learn here transfers to any tool.

::: tip Key takeaway
Visual design is how a product looks, and it should help users, not just decorate. Mockups are static hi-fi screens; a hi-fi prototype connects them so they can be tried out. Organize your Figma file with separate pages for each stage and clearly named, numbered frames for each screen.
:::
