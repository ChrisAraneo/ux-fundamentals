# Chapter 2: The UX design process

In [Chapter 1](../foundations/), you met the five phases of design thinking. This chapter takes a closer, practical look at the first three: **empathize**, **define**, and **ideate**. You will learn the tools that UX designers use every day to understand people, describe their problems, and come up with ideas to solve them.

Before you start, it helps to remember that these phases are not a straight line. You will often go back to an earlier step when you learn something new about your users.

## What you will learn

1. **How to show your work** - what a UX portfolio is and how to plan one from day one.
2. **How to understand users** - empathy, user interviews, and empathy maps.
3. **How to describe users and their problems** - pain points, personas, user stories, edge cases, and user journey maps.
4. **How to include everyone** - accessibility during research and the curb-cut effect.
5. **How to define the problem** - problem statements, hypothesis statements, and value propositions.
6. **How people think** - human factors and psychology principles that shape design.
7. **How to generate ideas** - ideation, goal statements, competitive audits, "How might we", and Crazy Eights.

## Running example: WashSlot

Most pages in this chapter follow one fictional project, so you can see how each tool builds on the previous one.

A **housing cooperative** manages an apartment building with 60 flats. All residents share one laundry room in the basement with four washing machines and two dryers. Today, residents book machines on a paper sheet pinned to the laundry room door. The sheet is often wet, torn, or ignored, and people regularly carry heavy baskets downstairs only to find every machine busy.

The cooperative hires a UX designer to create **WashSlot**, a simple app for booking machines and checking whether they are free. The cooperative has a small budget, wants the first version within four months, and insists that residents without smartphones must not be left out.

During the chapter, you will meet three personas:

- **Tomasz, 41** - a single father of two who works from home and does laundry almost every day.
- **Grace, 74** - a retired librarian who wears a hearing aid, uses large text on her tablet, and prefers talking to people over using apps.
- **Kofi, 23** - an international student who works evening shifts, does laundry late at night, and is still learning the local language.
